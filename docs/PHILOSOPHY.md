# CommonGround Social — Philosophy

## Why this exists

Every community platform built in the last decade optimized for the same thing: time on site. More notifications, more posts, more reasons to open the app again. The result is platforms that are profitable but corrosive — designed to replace real relationships with simulated ones.

CommonGround starts from the opposite premise. **The goal is for you to close the app.**

We measure success not by daily active users or session length, but by the number of times a neighbor borrowed a drill from someone they'd never met, or found someone to split a load of mulch with. Those are offline events. We never see them. That's the point.

---

## The four principles

### 1. Inverse Retention

Every feature must pass a single test: does this help someone reach an offline outcome, or does it keep them on the platform?

We will not build:
- Algorithmic feeds ranked by engagement
- Notifications optimized for re-engagement
- Like counts, comment counts, view counts
- "Suggested connections" based on activity patterns
- Streaks, badges, or any gamification of participation

We will build:
- A simple resource pool people can browse without an account
- A minimal connection request flow
- Email notifications that deliver information and stop

### 2. User-Owned Data

Your data belongs to you. Not in the legal boilerplate sense — in the operational sense.

- You can export everything you've ever listed or requested
- You can delete your account and all associated data, completely, no waiting period
- We do not sell, license, or analyze your data for advertising
- We do not build behavioral profiles
- Location data (exact lat/lng) is stored for proximity queries only and is never exposed, not even to other users

The three tiers of data we hold:
1. **Platform-retained** — legal/audit records (connection request metadata), retained for legitimate dispute resolution
2. **User-controlled** — your profile, your resource listings. You own it, you can delete it.
3. **Ephemeral by default** — nothing is "always on." Resources default to available; you mark them unavailable. No ambient presence.

### 3. Asymmetric Value Exchange

We take an 8% platform fee on funded community visions (Phase 3 feature). That's it. No premium tiers. No boosted listings. No advertising. No selling data.

The fee is public. The formula is public. There are no hidden revenue streams.

If we can't sustain operations on 8% of community-generated transactions, we will say so publicly and ask for support — the way Wikipedia does, not the way venture-backed startups do.

### 4. Demonetization of Core Mechanics

Connecting with your neighbor is free. Listing a resource is free. Browsing is free. Sending a connection request is free. These will never be monetized.

The value we capture is strictly proportional to economic activity we enable (community projects that receive funding). We do not charge for the social layer.

## What we refuse to build

- **Identity verification tied to behavior surveillance** — we may verify identities for funded projects, but not for browsing or connecting
- **Direct messaging** — intentional. Use the connection request to exchange contact info and move off-platform.
- **Photos of people as a requirement** — no profile photo required to sign up, list a resource, or make a connection. Optional profile photos may be added in a later version, but will never be used for filtering, sorting, or shown before a connection is made. Project and place photos are encouraged; they document what was built, not who built it.
- **Follower counts or reputation scores** — creates status hierarchies that replicate the problems we're trying to avoid
- **"Most active neighbor" rankings** — gamification of community participation
- **Advertising** — ever
- **Algorithmic curation** — the feed is chronological or proximity-based. No engagement ranking.

## Models we admire

| Organization | Why |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | Takes no VC money, builds for users not advertisers, open source, nonprofit |
| **Wikipedia** | Runs on donations, community-governed, no ads, transparent operations |
| **Craigslist** | Deliberately ugly, stubbornly resistant to "growth hacking," still works |
| **Mozilla** | Mission-driven, transparent financials, willing to say what they won't do |

We are not trying to be any of these exactly. But they prove that durable, useful, non-extractive technology is possible.

---

## The ethical baseline

We will not take venture capital funding. VC incentive structures are incompatible with what we're building — they require scale at the expense of mission, and eventual exit at the expense of users.

If this works, we want to structure it as a nonprofit or cooperative. The specifics are a legal question for later. The intention is fixed now.

We will publish our finances annually. We will publish our product roadmap. We will publish this document and update it when our thinking changes.

If we ever contradict any of this in our actual product behavior, we expect to be called out for it. This document is a public commitment, not a marketing exercise.
